Did the MAES deer run?

Every deer visit in the month after the sound pod came back: 31 of them, each with the pod's own video, the camera's full-resolution clip with its audio, and the exact moment each deterrent sound played. Watch any of them and judge for yourself; the note beside each one says what the video shows.

22 of 31visits where the pod played a sound while a deer was around.
6 of 7sounds that land inside a camera clip are loud on the camera's own microphone, +15 to +48 dB at the logged second. The sounds really played, when the pod says they did.
2deer that looked like they reacted: gone in 2 to 4 s, right after a blast the microphone recorded (V04, V15).
7deer that stayed 12 to 20 s or longer while sounds played. The clearest is V27.

What the video shows

Almost every deer that heard a sound was out of view within about a minute, but the video mostly doesn't show the sound driving them off. Deer here usually walk along the corn edge and step into the corn within about 10 seconds anyway: V06 and V07 did exactly that with no sound at all. Against that baseline, only two visits look like a real reaction. Seven deer stood their ground through the sound, and at night the pod's video is too faint to judge.

How to read a visit

  • Pod's video is the pod's own low-resolution copy of the camera (640×480). It starts the moment the pod detects something, and the sound fires about a second in. It has no audio.
  • Camera's own clip is the same camera recording on its own at 2304×1296, with sound, whenever its motion sensor trips. The shaded line under it is how loud the clip is.
  • An amber mark is a sound the pod played. Select it to play from just before. While a sound is playing, its name shows on the video.

How this was put together

A visit is one or more camera clips with a confident deer detection (0.9 or higher), grouped when they fall within 15 minutes of each other. Sound times come from the sound pod's own log, which records every sound it starts. The pod's video starts at the session start logged in the database, so each mark sits at the true offset, give or take a second. On the camera clips, the loudness line comes from the clip's own audio, so when a sound lands inside a clip you can check that the spike sits on the mark.

Limits: at night the pod's 640×480 infrared video is too faint to judge a reaction. V13's pod video was never uploaded and is gone from the pod. The camera clock read 1970 on Aug 20, so V01 and V02's camera clips can't be lined up with the sound. Close-ups come from the pod's own tracking boxes, or from the cloud's detections on the camera clips. The notes are judgements from these frames, and the videos are here so you can check them.
