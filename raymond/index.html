How often did deer come to the Raymond farm?

We watched every recording the barn and forest-view cameras made over three and a half days and checked each one by eye.

Deer came every day: 10 visits in three and a half days, mostly around dawn and dusk, crossing the lawn between the barn and the woods. Among them were a doe with a spotted fawn, a buck in velvet, and a group of three grazing for fifteen minutes.

Where and when a sound pod would help

  1. Put the first pod where the barn and forest-view cameras overlap. 6 of the 10 visits were seen by both cameras within seconds of each other: the deer use the same stretch of lawn between the barn and the woods. In both views they almost always came in from the left and left to the right, so aim the pod along that path, toward the tree line they step out of.
  2. Run it 4:30–7:30 am and 5:00–9:00 pm. Nine of the ten visits fell in those two windows. One visit was at 11 pm, so a quieter night mode is worth adding once the pod is proven.
  3. Only react to confirmed deer. Squirrels, foxes, dogs and insects set the cameras off far more often than deer. A pod that fires on motion alone would sound all day.
  4. Change the sounds up. This is a resident family (the same spotted fawn came back four times in one day), so the same deer will hear the pod night after night. Rotating sounds keeps them from getting used to it.
  5. Keep recording into the fall. Three and a half days shows the pattern, but deer movement picks up in October and November, and a longer record will show whether the path and times hold.

How we checked

Every recording was checked. The farm's AI box records whenever its own detector sees something move. We re-ran the AI Sense platform's detection models (MegaDetector and SpeciesNet) on every recording, then reviewed each one by eye using a summary image: the best frame, six frames spread across the whole recording, and close-ups.

Every deer call was checked twice. Anything called "deer" or "unclear" went to a second, independent reviewer asked to prove it wasn't a deer. We then looked again ourselves at the most important ones: the buck, the group of three, and the one call that was thrown out (a parked trailer).

Counts and times. Deer counts are the most deer both reviewers could see at the same moment. Times come from the AI box's clock; the time printed on each camera's picture can differ by a few minutes.

Limits. The cameras only record when the AI box's detector fires, so a deer that never set it off isn't here. Three and a half days is a short window. Recordings that include people are not shown.
